PREVENTION

Protecting your oral health

Prevention focuses on reducing risk, maintaining healthy habits and identifying potential problems early.

WHY PREVENTION?

Prevention is the foundation of oral health.

Many oral diseases develop gradually. Preventive care aims to reduce exposure to risk factors and support the natural protective mechanisms of the mouth.

Good oral health is not based on one single habit. It results from several behaviours working together, including oral hygiene, appropriate fluoride exposure, healthy dietary habits and professional dental care.

DAILY HABITS

Six habits that support oral health

01

Brush your teeth

Brush regularly using fluoride toothpaste and make sure tooth surfaces are cleaned carefully.

02

Clean between teeth

Interdental cleaning helps remove plaque and debris from areas that may be difficult to reach with a toothbrush.

03

Limit frequent sugar exposure

Reducing frequent exposure to free sugars can help reduce conditions associated with dental caries.

04

Choose water

Water is a simple alternative to sugar-sweetened beverages and can reduce unnecessary sugar exposure.

05

Use fluoride appropriately

Fluoride can help strengthen enamel and support remineralisation.

06

Visit a dental professional

Professional care allows oral-health problems to be identified and personalised advice to be provided.

DIET

How does diet affect teeth?

Diet can influence the oral environment. Foods and drinks containing free sugars can provide substrates that oral microorganisms metabolise.

The frequency of exposure is particularly important because repeated sugar exposure can lead to repeated periods of acid production.

A balanced diet and choosing water more often can therefore be part of a broader preventive strategy.

SUGARY DRINKS

Why should we pay attention to beverages?

🥤 Sugar-Sweetened Drinks

Soft drinks, sweetened teas and other sugary beverages can contribute to frequent exposure to free sugars.

Consider frequency of consumption

💧 Water

Water does not contain added sugars and can be a simple everyday beverage choice.

Simple preventive choice

COMMON MISTAKES

Oral hygiene mistakes to avoid

01 — Brushing too quickly

Rushing can make it easier to miss certain tooth surfaces.

02 — Ignoring between teeth

A toothbrush does not clean every area between teeth.

03 — Frequent sugary snacks

Repeated sugar exposure can increase the number of acid challenges experienced by teeth.

04 — Avoiding dental care

Delaying professional assessment can allow some problems to progress without being noticed.

A REALISTIC ROUTINE

A simple daily approach

Morning

Brush your teeth with fluoride toothpaste.

During the day

Choose water regularly and be mindful of frequent sugary drinks.

Evening

Clean your teeth carefully and include interdental cleaning.

Regularly

Attend dental appointments according to professional advice.

MYTH VS FACT

Common dental misconceptions

MYTH

“If a tooth does not hurt, it must be completely healthy.”

FACT

Some dental problems can develop without obvious symptoms, which is one reason professional dental care matters.

MYTH

“Only candy can contribute to dental caries.”

FACT

Many foods and drinks can contain fermentable carbohydrates, so overall dietary patterns matter.
