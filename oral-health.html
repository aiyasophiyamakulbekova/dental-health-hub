ORAL HEALTH

Understanding the mouth

Explore the structure of teeth, common oral diseases and the biological processes that influence oral health.

THE BASICS

What is oral health?

Oral health refers to the condition of the mouth, including the teeth, gums and supporting tissues. A healthy mouth allows a person to eat, speak and communicate without pain or significant discomfort.

Oral health is influenced by biological, behavioural, environmental and social factors. Oral hygiene, diet, fluoride exposure, access to professional care and individual susceptibility can all play a role.

TOOTH ANATOMY

What is a tooth made of?

A tooth consists of several specialised tissues. Each part has a different role in protecting the tooth, supporting its structure or allowing it to respond to external stimuli.

01

Enamel

Enamel is the outermost layer of the crown. It is highly mineralised and provides protection against mechanical forces and chemical challenges.

02

Dentin

Dentin lies beneath enamel and forms much of the tooth's internal structure. It is less mineralised than enamel.

03

Pulp

The dental pulp is located in the centre of the tooth. It contains nerves, blood vessels and connective tissue.

04

Cementum

Cementum covers the tooth root and helps connect the tooth to the periodontal ligament.

05

Periodontal Ligament

This specialised connective tissue helps attach the tooth to the surrounding bone and contributes to sensory function.

06

Gingiva

The gingiva, or gums, surrounds the teeth and forms part of the protective tissues of the oral cavity.

COMMON DISEASES

Oral diseases

01

Dental Caries

Dental caries is a disease involving the progressive loss of minerals from dental hard tissues. Microorganisms within dental plaque can metabolise fermentable carbohydrates and produce acids.

Repeated acid exposure can shift the balance toward demineralisation. Without sufficient repair, the lesion may progress and damage the tooth.

02

Gingivitis

Gingivitis is inflammation of the gingival tissues and is commonly associated with dental plaque accumulating near the gumline.

Possible signs include redness, swelling and bleeding. Effective plaque control can help improve gingival health.

03

Periodontal Disease

Periodontal diseases affect the tissues supporting the teeth. Persistent inflammation can lead to damage of periodontal structures.

Professional assessment is important when periodontal disease is suspected.

04

Dental Erosion

Dental erosion is the chemical loss of tooth structure caused by acids that are not produced by bacteria.

Frequent exposure to acidic foods and drinks may contribute to erosive tooth wear.

05

Tooth Sensitivity

Tooth sensitivity can occur when dentin becomes more exposed. Cold, hot, sweet or acidic stimuli may then cause discomfort.

Persistent sensitivity should be discussed with a dental professional because different causes are possible.

PLAQUE

What is dental plaque?

Dental plaque is a structured microbial biofilm that develops on tooth surfaces. It contains microorganisms embedded in a matrix and can interact with dietary components.

When plaque is not adequately controlled, it can contribute to processes involved in dental caries and gingival inflammation.

RISK FACTORS

What can increase oral health risks?

Frequent sugar exposure

Frequent exposure to fermentable carbohydrates can support conditions associated with dental caries.

Poor plaque control

Inadequate removal of plaque can contribute to caries and gingival inflammation.

Acidic drinks

Frequent exposure to dietary acids can contribute to erosive tooth wear.

Irregular dental care

Professional dental care can help identify problems and provide personalised preventive advice.

WHEN TO SEEK HELP

When should you talk to a dentist?

Professional dental advice is important when symptoms persist or when you notice changes in your teeth or gums.

✓ Persistent tooth pain

✓ Swelling around the mouth or gums

✓ Repeated gum bleeding

✓ Persistent sensitivity

✓ Changes in the appearance of a tooth

✓ Any oral problem that concerns you
