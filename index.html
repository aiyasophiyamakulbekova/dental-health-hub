INTRODUCTION

Why does oral health matter?

Oral health is an important part of overall health and well-being. Teeth and gums allow us to eat, speak and communicate comfortably. Oral diseases can affect daily life, confidence and quality of life.

Many common oral diseases are preventable. Learning how dental diseases develop can help people understand the importance of prevention and make informed decisions about their oral health.

Dentistry combines biology, chemistry, medicine, technology and patient care. This website explores these areas from a scientific and educational perspective.

KEY FACTORS

What affects oral health?

01

Oral Hygiene

Regular toothbrushing and cleaning between teeth help control dental plaque and support healthy teeth and gums.

02

Diet

Dietary habits influence oral health. Frequent exposure to free sugars can contribute to conditions associated with dental caries.

03

Fluoride

Fluoride helps protect enamel and supports the natural remineralisation process.

04

Dental Care

Professional dental care can help identify problems and provide individual preventive advice.

05

Habits

Daily habits, including brushing, dietary choices and interdental cleaning, can influence long-term oral health.

06

Education

Understanding the science of oral diseases can make preventive behaviour more meaningful.

DENTISTRY & SCIENCE

A multidisciplinary field

Dentistry is closely connected to anatomy, physiology, microbiology, chemistry and public health. Dentists need to understand how tissues develop, how diseases progress and how treatments affect the body.

Modern dentistry also uses digital technologies, imaging, biomaterials and computer-assisted techniques. This makes dentistry both a healthcare profession and a constantly developing scientific field.

Preventive dentistry is especially important because preventing disease can reduce the need for more complex treatment later.

FROM PLAQUE TO CARIES

How can dental caries develop?

01

Dental plaque forms

Microorganisms and other components can accumulate on tooth surfaces and form a dental biofilm.

02

Sugars are metabolised

Certain microorganisms can use fermentable carbohydrates and produce acids.

03

Mineral loss occurs

Acidic conditions can contribute to demineralisation of enamel.

04

The process may progress

If mineral loss repeatedly exceeds repair, a carious lesion can develop.

DID YOU KNOW?

Quick dental facts

01

Enamel is the highly mineralised outer layer of a tooth.

02

Dental plaque is a microbial biofilm that can accumulate on tooth surfaces.

03

Fluoride can help strengthen enamel and support remineralisation.

04

Frequent exposure to free sugars is an important consideration in caries prevention.

ABOUT THE PROJECT

Why did I create Dental Health Hub?

This project was created to explore dentistry beyond the traditional image of a dental clinic. The goal is to understand the scientific principles behind common oral diseases and communicate reliable information in an accessible way.

The website also connects dental education with independent research. One of its main topics is the relationship between sugar-sweetened beverage consumption and dental health among teenagers.

Through this project, I aim to develop my knowledge of dentistry, scientific research, data analysis and health communication.
